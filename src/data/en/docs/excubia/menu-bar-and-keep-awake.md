Excubia lives in the menu bar. Click its icon to open the panel; in **Block everything** the shield is crossed out.

## At the top

The name, the filter state and the **mode menu**, with the four modes and, if you have any, the rule sets. An orange cup means the Mac is being kept awake.

## Status tab

1. **Alerts waiting**: if an alert ended up behind other windows, **Show** brings it back to the front.
2. **Protection**: the filter switch, the mode and active rule set, and the counters of allowed and blocked connections.
3. **Block everything**: the switch and the **Block everything for a while** menu (15 min, 30 min, 1 hour, 2 hours). During timed blocking, the end time and **End now** appear. Turning the switch off restores the previous mode, or Ask.
4. **Learning**, if it's running, with **Stop and review…**.
5. **Network**: **Download** and **Upload** speed, a graph of the last minute and the traffic **Since this Mac started**.
6. **Data usage**: the three apps that use the most.

## Activity tab

The apps that connected recently, with the number of allowed and blocked connections; open a row to see the destinations.

## At the bottom

**Open Excubia** opens the window. **Quit** closes the app after a confirmation: rules, blocklists and the mode stay active in the filter, but in Ask mode new connections go through without an alert until you reopen Excubia.

## Speed instead of the icon

In **Settings › General › Menu bar** you can show the **Icon**, the **Speed** (download and upload as numbers, on two lines) or a **Graph** of the last 30 seconds.

## Keep Awake

With **Keep Awake in the menu** on (Settings › General), the panel gets a third tab:

- **Keep this Mac awake**, for a chosen time: indefinitely, 15 or 30 minutes, or 1, 2, 3, 5 or 8 hours. Excubia remembers the last duration.
- **Allow the display to sleep**: the Mac stays on but the display turns off as usual (off at first).
- **Stop on low battery**: on Macs with a battery, turns off below 20% when running on battery (on at first).

Closing the lid still puts the Mac to sleep, unless it's connected to power and an external display. Keep Awake doesn't change any system setting and stops when you quit Excubia.
